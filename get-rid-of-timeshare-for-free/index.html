How to get rid of a timeshare for free

By Andrea · Last updated: August 2026

Yes, there are genuinely free ways to get out of a timeshare. But they are limited, they depend on your specific contract and state, and they don't work for everyone. It also helps to know this up front: many offers that promise to remove your timeshare "for free" are not free at all. Some are outright scams. This page walks through the honest options, and the traps to avoid.

A quick note before we begin. This is information for consumers, not legal, financial, or tax advice. We are not a law firm, an exit company, or a resort, and we don't sell any service. Rules vary by state and by contract. Always verify the details with your resort or developer, a licensed attorney in your state, and the Federal Trade Commission at consumer.ftc.gov.

The genuinely free (or near-free) ways out

These are the paths most likely to cost you little or nothing. Whether any of them fits depends on your contract and your situation.

  • Cancel within your rescission window. If you bought recently, you may still be inside the legal cancellation period.
  • Ask the developer for a deed-back or take-back. Some resorts have a program that lets qualifying owners hand the timeshare back.
  • Transfer or give it to a willing party. If someone actually wants it, a legitimate transfer may end your obligation.
  • Sell it, even for $1. On resale marketplaces, a token price is sometimes what it takes to move it.

1. Cancel within your rescission window

If your purchase is recent, the fastest and cheapest exit is rescission, which means canceling the contract under the law. The number of days you have is not universal. It depends on your state and your contract, so check both carefully and don't rely on a figure you read somewhere else. If you're still inside that window, act quickly and follow the exact instructions in your paperwork. Our guide to writing a timeshare cancellation letter can help you do this in writing, which is usually required.

2. Ask the developer for a deed-back or take-back

Some developers run a program that takes the timeshare back from owners who qualify. It is not guaranteed, and eligibility rules vary, but for owners who are current on payments and fees it can be a clean, low-cost exit. It costs nothing to ask. Start by contacting your resort directly and asking whether they have a deed-back or surrender program. Learn more on our timeshare deed-back page.

3. Transfer or give it to a willing party

If a family member, friend, or another buyer genuinely wants the timeshare, you may be able to transfer it to them. A legitimate transfer has to be recorded properly and accepted by the resort to actually release you from the obligation. Get it in writing and confirm with the resort that your name is off the deed and the account.

4. Sell it, even for $1

Owners are often surprised that some timeshares list for a single dollar on resale marketplaces. That can feel discouraging, but for a hard-to-move week it may simply be the market price. Reputable resale sites let you list without large upfront fees. Be patient, be honest in your listing, and keep your expectations realistic.

A reality check on resale value

Here is the honest part. Many timeshares sell for very little, and some sell for nothing at all. That is normal. A timeshare is usually not an investment that holds value, and the resale market for most of them is thin. If someone tells you your timeshare is worth thousands and they have a buyer lined up, be very skeptical. That promise is one of the most common signs of a scam. A fair, free exit is often worth more to you than chasing a resale price that isn't real.

The "free" offers that are actually scams

Because so many owners want out, a whole industry has grown up around that desire, and not all of it is honest. Watch for these patterns.

  • "We'll take it off your hands for free" with an upfront fee. If it's truly free, why is there a charge before anything happens? Large upfront fees are a red flag.
  • Resale companies charging big upfront listing fees. Legitimate resale platforms rarely require hundreds or thousands of dollars just to list.
  • Anyone guaranteeing a buyer or a specific price. No honest party can guarantee that your timeshare will sell, or for how much.
  • Pressure to act "today" or lose the deal. Urgency is a sales tactic, not a favor to you.

Before you pay anyone, check them out. Look up the company with the FTC at consumer.ftc.gov and read reviews and complaint history at the Better Business Bureau (bbb.org). If you're pressured to wire money or pay before any work is done, stop.

If free options don't work

Sometimes the free routes just don't apply. Your rescission window may have closed, your resort may not offer a deed-back, and no buyer may appear. If that's where you are, don't panic and don't rush into the first company that calls you. Start by reading our overview of how to get out of a timeshare, which lays out the full range of options in order.

Some owners consider a paid exit company. Approach this cautiously. There are legitimate professionals and there are bad actors, and it can be hard to tell them apart. Our page on timeshare exit companies explains what to look for and what to avoid. We don't endorse any specific company, and for anything involving your contract or your money, it's wise to talk to a licensed attorney in your state first.

Frequently asked questions

Can I really get rid of my timeshare for free?

Sometimes, yes. Canceling within your rescission window, a developer deed-back, a genuine transfer, or a low-price resale can all cost little or nothing. But none of these is guaranteed, and whether any applies depends on your contract and your state. It costs nothing to check each one.

Will the developer take it back for free?

Some developers offer a deed-back or surrender program for qualifying owners, and some don't. Eligibility often depends on being current on payments and fees. The only way to know is to ask your resort directly. There's no universal rule, so confirm the details with them in writing.

Is giving my timeshare away legal?

In general, transferring a timeshare to a willing party is a normal, legal thing to do, but it has to be done correctly. The transfer usually must be recorded and accepted by the resort for you to actually be released from the obligation. Because the paperwork matters, consider having a licensed attorney review it so your name really does come off the deed and account.

Why is the resale value so low?

Most timeshares don't hold value, and the resale market for them is small. Ongoing maintenance fees also make many weeks hard to give away, let alone sell for a profit. A low or even $1 price is common and not a sign that you did something wrong. Be wary of anyone who claims otherwise.

Whatever path you take, move at your own pace, keep everything in writing, and verify the specifics with your resort, a licensed attorney, and the FTC before you pay anyone anything.
