Wesley Financial Group: what to know

By Andrea · Last updated: August 2026

If you're researching Wesley Financial Group, here's a neutral overview of what the company appears to offer and, more importantly, how to evaluate any timeshare exit company before you pay a single dollar. What Wesley Financial Group appears to be

Based on how it presents itself publicly, Wesley Financial Group is a US company that markets timeshare exit and cancellation services to owners who want out of their contracts. In general terms, companies like this position themselves as advocates who work on your behalf to try to end your timeshare obligation.

We're keeping this description deliberately general. Company details, pricing, ownership, and standing can change over time, so please confirm the current facts yourself on the company's own website and through independent, third-party sources before making any decision. Do not rely on this page, or any single page, as the final word.

How timeshare exit companies like this work

Most timeshare exit companies charge a fee to try to get you released from your contract. They may negotiate with your resort or developer, help you document complaints, or guide you through a cancellation process. What they generally cannot do is promise a result.

Outcomes and timelines vary widely. Two owners with similar contracts can have very different experiences depending on the developer, the contract terms, the state, and the specific facts. Some cases resolve; others do not. For a fuller picture of how this industry operates and what to watch for, see our overview of timeshare exit companies.

How to evaluate before you sign or pay

Before you commit to Wesley Financial Group, or any exit company, slow down and do your own homework:

  • Check the company on the Better Business Bureau (BBB) and read how it handles complaints, not just its rating.
  • Review consumer guidance from the FTC at consumer.ftc.gov and search your state attorney general for any actions or advisories.
  • Prefer arrangements that use escrow or that do not require a large upfront fee, so your money isn't fully at risk before any work is done.
  • Get a written scope, timeline, and refund terms in plain language. If it isn't in writing, treat it as if it wasn't promised.
  • Be wary of any guarantee of cancellation. No one can honestly promise a specific legal outcome.
  • Read independent reviews from multiple sources, and look at the negative reviews as closely as the positive ones.

When you search "wesley financial group reviews," remember that reviews can be incomplete, curated, or outdated. Use them as one input, not proof.

General red flags of exit-company scams. These apply to the whole industry, not any one company. Be cautious if you see: a guarantee that your timeshare will be canceled; pressure to sign or pay quickly; a large upfront fee with no escrow; a request to stop paying your mortgage or maintenance fees without clear written advice; vague or missing refund terms; or claims that sound too good to be true. When in doubt, walk away and verify.

Cheaper and free options to try first

Paying an exit company is not your only path, and it is rarely the first thing to try. Before you spend thousands of dollars, consider lower-cost and free options:

  • Start with the basics in our guide on how to get out of a timeshare, including checking whether your rescission window still applies.
  • Ask your developer or resort about a timeshare deed-back or surrender program, which some resorts offer at little or no cost.
  • If your situation involves possible fraud or misrepresentation, consider speaking with a timeshare lawyer about your specific contract before paying an exit company.

Rescission rights and cancellation laws vary by state and by contract, so there are no universal numbers or deadlines. Read your own contract and confirm the current rules for your state.

Frequently asked questions

We can't make that call for you, and it wouldn't be responsible to. Legitimacy and current standing are things you should verify yourself. How much do timeshare exit companies cost?

Costs vary and are often in the thousands of dollars, but there is no fixed or standard price across the industry. Always get the full fee, payment structure, and refund terms in writing, and ask whether the payment is held in escrow until work is completed.

No company can honestly guarantee a specific legal outcome. Cancellation depends on your contract, your developer, your state's laws, and the facts of your case. Treat any guarantee of cancellation as a warning sign.
